Sources & methodology

How the register connects claims to evidence.

The Public Knowledge Register keeps source identity, public access routes, evidence limits and rights treatment separate so a visitor can understand what is documented and what remains open.

Source identity

The source is the publication, organization, archive or submitted record responsible for the indexed occurrence. A hosting or discovery platform is not silently presented as the source.

Access route

The access route is where a visitor can currently reach the material. Page-level links are used when an indexed page is known; otherwise the interface clearly labels an issue-level or source-level route.

Evidence boundary

Records state what the available evidence supports and what has not yet been verified. Pending visual comparison does not erase a usable indexed record, but it limits claims about exact wording, layout and visual identity.

Rights boundary

The register indexes and paraphrases narrowly. It does not reproduce protected historical scans merely because they can be reached elsewhere.
