BRAND-GT-DYNO

GT and Dyno Research Dossier

A catalog-controlled research collection separating origins, acquisition, design credit, model families, serial systems and artifact relationships.

The GT and Dyno dossier separates four commonly collapsed events: Gary Turner’s first GT frame in 1972, Bob Morales’s creation of Dyno as a soft-goods and accessories brand in 1982, the first GT Performer design in 1983 and GT’s acquisition of Dyno in 1985. Catalog and serial conclusions remain year-, market-, model- and factory-specific.

What the Evidence Supports

Qualified conclusionDate

GT Bicycles was a fully formed bicycle company in 1972.

Approved public wording: GT traces its engineering origin to Gary Turner’s first frame in 1972; the company and business partnership developed later in the 1970s.

Why this status: Current official history uses the first frame as the origin marker, while the fuller business chronology is later.

What this does not prove: The first frame date is not automatically the legal founding date of the later company.

Sources: About GT

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimDate

Dyno began selling bicycles in 1982.

Approved public wording: Dyno began in 1982 as an apparel and accessories brand and entered bicycles after GT acquired it in 1985.

Why this status: Morales identifies the early Dyno products as soft goods and accessories, then describes the first Dyno frame and fork after the sale to GT.

Sources: Bob Morales History, National BMX Hall of Fame — Bob Morales

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimIdentity

The 1983 GT Performer was a Dyno bicycle.

Approved public wording: The first GT Performer was a GT freestyle design developed by Bob Morales and Eddie Fiola in 1983, before GT acquired Dyno.

Why this status: The first-person history places the design before the 1985 acquisition.

Sources: Bob Morales History

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimRelationship

GT and Dyno became interchangeable brand names after 1985.

Approved public wording: GT and Dyno remained distinct product brands under shared corporate ownership.

Why this status: Separate model families, catalogs and branding continued after the acquisition.

Sources: Bob Morales History, GT Catalog Reconstruction Index, Dyno Catalog Reconstruction Index

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimSpecification

A model name such as Performer or Compe determines one universal specification.

Approved public wording: GT and Dyno specifications must be stated from the exact model year, market, factory and catalog source.

Why this status: Materials, geometry, brakes, components and factory codes changed while model names continued.

Sources: GT Catalog Reconstruction Index, Dyno Catalog Reconstruction Index

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimSpecification

All GT and Dyno serials follow one universal date formula.

Approved public wording: GT and Dyno serial interpretation is factory-, period- and model-specific; literal prefixes, marking locations and catalog features must be evaluated together.

Why this status: The register documents multiple GT and Dyno factory patterns, including USA sequences and prefixes such as YC, DH, SY and SK.

What this does not prove: A single forum decoder or partial serial can authenticate every bicycle.

Sources: GT Catalog Reconstruction Index, Dyno Catalog Reconstruction Index

What Remains Open

What are the exact corporate milestones and legal entities in the GT/Dyno acquisition history?

Why it matters: It would separate engineering origin, partnership, incorporation and acquisition precisely.

Next evidence: Corporate filings, period trade reporting and first-party records. P0

Which designers and factories controlled each later GT and Dyno model?

Why it matters: It prevents blanket design and manufacturing attribution.

Next evidence: Patents, catalogs, factory records, interviews and complete markings. P0

Can the historic GT/Dyno BMXMuseum catalog reference IDs and editions be recovered?

Why it matters: They would improve canonical bibliographic links without copying scans.

Next evidence: Ordinary public backlinks, catalog citations and collector indexes. P0

Timeline

  1. 1972

    Gary Turner built the first frame in the GT engineering lineage.

    Origin marker, not complete corporate history.
  2. 1982

    Bob Morales created Dyno as an apparel and accessories brand.

    Not yet a bicycle brand.
  3. 1983

    Morales and Eddie Fiola developed GT’s first Performer freestyle frame.

    GT design before the Dyno acquisition.
  4. 1985

    GT acquired Dyno; Morales then designed the first Dyno frame and fork.

    Shared ownership, distinct brands.
  5. 1986

    Morales left full-time GT work and continued in a more limited consulting role.

    Later design credit must be model-specific.
  6. 1991–1999

    Reconstructed catalogs document changing GT and Dyno model families, materials and serial/factory patterns.

    Catalog-controlled, not one universal specification.
  7. 2012

    Official reporting verified Felicia Stancil’s two Junior Women world titles for Factory GT.

    Achievement proof remains separate from artifact authentication.

Relationships

What this does not prove: The design predates GT’s acquisition of Dyno and is not a Dyno bicycle.
