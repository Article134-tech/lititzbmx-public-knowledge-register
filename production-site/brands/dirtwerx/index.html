BRAND-DIRTWERX

DIRTWERX

Formation, practical launch, products, distribution and team history are kept as separate dated claims.

DIRTWERX is documented through a contemporary newspaper, Harry Leary’s first-person company interview, official later summaries and period-publication leads. Current evidence supports formation around 1995, practical operations around 1996 and a detailed March 2000 snapshot of products, sales, exports and the Sycamore BMX-centered team.

What the Evidence Supports

Qualified conclusionDate

DIRTWERX was founded on an exact date in 1996.

Approved public wording: DIRTWERX formed around 1995 and began practical operations around 1996; the exact legal founding date remains unresolved.

Why this status: A June 1997 newspaper places formation about two years earlier, while Leary’s 2000 interview says the business basically started in 1996. The two sources support a formation-versus-operational-launch distinction.

What this does not prove: Neither source establishes an exact legal filing date or a single day on which every business activity began.

Sources: Teaching BMX Racing’s Fine Points, Harry Leary — Founder Interview

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

AttributedSpecification

DIRTWERX offered a nine-size chromoly frame family, race forks and handlebars by March 2000.

Approved public wording: In March 2000, Leary described a DIRTWERX product line of chromoly frames in nine named sizes, race forks and handlebars.

Why this status: The founder interview lists Mini, Jr, Expert, Expert XL, Pro, Pro XL, XXL-Stilt, 24-XL and 24-XXL Stilt, plus forks and handlebars.

What this does not prove: The 2000 snapshot does not establish every earlier prototype, later model, geometry, fabricator or universal feature.

Sources: Harry Leary — Founder Interview

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

AttributedContext

DIRTWERX sold nationally, directly and internationally in 2000.

Approved public wording: Leary said DIRTWERX sold through Dan’s, bicycle shops across the United States and direct, with exports to Japan, France, Mexico, Brazil and Singapore.

Why this status: The first-person interview names the sales routes and five export countries.

What this does not prove: Shipment volume, distributor contracts, exact dates and continuous availability are not established.

Sources: Harry Leary — Founder Interview

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimRelationship

The March 2000 DIRTWERX roster was a timeless nationwide factory-team roster.

Approved public wording: The March 2000 roster is a dated, local, Sycamore BMX-centered team snapshot from Leary’s own account.

Why this status: Leary named riders, classes and family/track relationships in one interview at one point in time.

What this does not prove: The roster cannot be projected to all regions, years or every rider ever supported by DIRTWERX.

Sources: Harry Leary — Founder Interview

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimRelationship

Greg Hill was a founder or co-owner of DIRTWERX.

Approved public wording: Leary credited Greg Hill as one of the people who encouraged him to start DIRTWERX; current evidence does not identify Hill as a founder, co-founder or owner.

Why this status: The founder interview describes encouragement, not shared ownership or formation authority.

Sources: Harry Leary — Founder Interview
