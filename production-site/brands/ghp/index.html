BRAND-GHP

Greg Hill Products (GHP)

Period sources support the 1983 launch while product, team, distribution and later revival phases remain separately qualified.

Greg Hill Products is supported by period publishing, magazine reporting and advertising. The strongest chronology places public operation and launch in 1983, commercial frame-and-fork availability by early 1984 and a June 1984 distribution relationship involving CyclePro and West Coast Cycle. Later operating phases remain secondary until stronger period or first-party records are recovered.

What the Evidence Supports

VerifiedDate

GHP launched in 1983.

Approved public wording: Multiple period sources support 1983 as the launch year for Greg Hill Products.

Why this status: A 1983 GHP-published book proves the company name was active, October 1983 reporting documents Hill’s transition to GHP, and a February 1984 profile says the company launched the previous year.

What this does not prove: The exact month, legal entity and filing date remain unresolved.

Sources: Greg Hill’s Professional BMX Skills, BMX Action Bike — October 1983, BMX Action Bike — February 1984

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

VerifiedSpecification

GHP frame-and-fork products were commercially available by early 1984.

Approved public wording: Period retail advertising listed Greg Hill frame-and-fork products by February 1984.

Why this status: The period advertisement establishes commercial availability.

What this does not prove: It does not establish exact geometry, model name, factory or complete component specification.

Sources: BMX Action Bike — February 1984

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

VerifiedRelationship

West Coast Cycle distributed GHP and Greg Hill CyclePro products in June 1984.

Approved public wording: A June 1984 advertisement stated that GHP and Greg Hill CyclePro products were distributed exclusively by West Coast Cycle.

Why this status: The relationship is directly stated in period advertising.

What this does not prove: Distribution does not establish that West Coast Cycle owned GHP, fabricated every product or controlled all territories and years.

Sources: BMX Action — June 1984

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Rejected claimRelationship

GHP, CyclePro and West Coast Cycle were one interchangeable company or manufacturer.

Approved public wording: Period sources establish team, product and distribution relationships among GHP, CyclePro and West Coast Cycle; ownership and fabrication roles remain distinct and partly unresolved.

Why this status: The advertising language supports a commercial relationship but does not collapse the entities.

Sources: BMX Action Bike — February 1984, BMX Action — June 1984

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

Qualified conclusionDate

GHP operated continuously from 1983 to the present.

Approved public wording: A later specialist history describes operating phases in 1983–1986, 1991–1993 and from about 2005 onward; those later phases remain secondary chronology hypotheses.

Why this status: The phase structure is useful but has not yet been controlled by complete period catalogs, corporate records or first-person history.

What this does not prove: A secondary phase summary does not establish uninterrupted legal or manufacturing continuity.

Sources: GHP BMX 2014 Pro XL Frame Review, Greg Hill Products

Last reviewed 2026-08-02 · Permalink to this claim

What Remains Open

Who fabricated each early GHP frame and fork, and how did CyclePro fit into production?

Why it matters: It controls maker attribution and model identification.

Next evidence: Original catalogs, invoices, manufacturing records, patents or first-person testimony. P0

What is the complete 1983–1986 model and team chronology?

Why it matters: It would separate model names, team periods and catalog specifications.

Next evidence: Period catalogs, advertisements and complete magazine pages. P0

Can the 1991–1993 and 2005-onward phases be independently documented?

Why it matters: It would establish whether the secondary operating-phase summary is accurate.

Next evidence: Period product literature, corporate records and Greg Hill interviews. P1
